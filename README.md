Across the entire 2017–2023 period, “Managing an Account” was the leading complaint issue, suggesting that account-management processes represent a major area requiring operational attention.

Dataset Limitations
There are several limitations to consider.
1. Complaint volume does not represent complaint rate
A product with more customers will naturally have the potential to generate more complaints.
Therefore, 24,814 complaints from Checking/Savings Accounts does not necessarily mean that the product has the poorest customer experience.

2. The dataset only contains reported complaints
Not every dissatisfied customer submits a formal complaint.
Therefore, the dataset may not represent the full level of customer dissatisfaction.

3. Limited information about root causes
The dataset identifies complaint categories and issues, but it may not fully explain why customers experienced those problems.

4. Missing customer information
Additional demographic and customer-level information could provide greater insight into who is experiencing particular problems.

5. External factors
Changes in the economy, regulations, banking technology and customer behaviour could have affected complaint volumes between 2017 and 2023.
The dataset alone cannot determine the impact of these external factors.

It is about identifying patterns, understanding what those patterns mean and translating the findings into actionable business recommendations. I learned how to analyse complaint trends across different years, months, products, issues and submission channels. I also learned the importance of looking beyond complaint volumes and considering factors such as response time, root causes and complaint rates.
